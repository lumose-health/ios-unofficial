# PRD Quality Review — GlycemicGPT for iOS and Apple Watch

*Reviewed 2026-08-03 against `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Artifacts: `prd.md` (144,703 words, 237 FRs, 17 sections), `addendum.md`, with spot-checks against `research/` and `sections/`.*

## Overall verdict

This is a PRD that earns its length. Across 237 FRs it does the hard thing consistently — every requirement carries testable consequences with real numbers, every divergence from the Android product is enumerated in a 136-row Parity Ledger with a named disclosure surface, and the document repeatedly chooses to state a loss rather than let it disappear into an implementation detail (PL-23's silenceable urgent low, §9.4's single hardware validator, NFR-10's refusal to inherit the Android battery figure). The safety architecture is genuinely load-bearing: SI-1 through SI-12 each carry an enforcement mechanism, and the two invariants most likely to break under an idiomatic Swift translation (SI-2's `precondition` trap, SI-12's synthesized `Codable`) are called out before any feature is described.

What is at risk is not the thinking but the scaffolding around it. The document's single most-cited authority — "settled decision N", invoked 48 times across nine distinct IDs including the ones that carry the Critical Alerts posture, the no-conveyed-binary rule and the lead developer's lack of hardware — is defined nowhere in this PRD, its addendum, or its memlog. Alongside that sit two smaller ID-hygiene failures in §14 and §15 that matter more than they would elsewhere, because this PRD's credibility rests on exactly the kind of self-audit that both of them get wrong.

Nothing here blocks a green light. The findings are traceability repairs, not rethinks.

## Decision-readiness — strong

A decision-maker can act on this. Decisions are stated as decisions and the cost of each is named. §12.2's deferral table gives every one of D-1 through D-9 a *revisit condition* rather than a vague "later" — D-9's is exemplary ("the bundled set has shipped through one tagged release **and** import, transcode and delivery of a real low alarm can be validated end to end at Validation Tier 4"). §7.2's PD table splits deliberate divergences into **fix** and **improvement** and includes rows marked **—** that record behaviour deliberately left unchanged (PD-14's severity-banding edge case) precisely so nobody "fixes" it on one platform alone. That is the opposite of smoothing to neutral.

The open questions are genuinely open. OQ-24 is the strongest example in the document: "does the Coverage Claim say 'watching' or 'watching, but you may not hear it'? The strict reading of SI-6 argues for the caveat, but it would appear for nearly every Builder nearly all the time, which risks training people to ignore the one surface whose entire purpose is to be believed." Both horns are stated, neither is resolved, and FR-66 records where the current draft sits pending a ruling. The seven questions marked **(blocking)** each name the work they gate rather than blocking the whole build.

The `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts land at real tensions, not safe checkpoints. §7.4's "Forced losses whose disclosure is carried by the wrong requirement" lists PL-3, PL-7, PL-38, PL-48, PL-49 as recorded with no user-facing disclosure and asks the PM to confirm that reading — a note that invites a "no". §7.4 also flags PL-45 (the draggable meal button) as "the one row in the forced-loss table that is a platform-fit choice rather than a hard platform bar. Confirm whether it belongs in PL or in PD." A PRD covering for itself would not do that.

What holds it back from being unimpeachable is provenance, not content — see the first finding below.

### Findings

- **high** Undefined decision register (§0–§16, 48 citations) — The document cites "settled decision 1", "decision 3", "settled decision 4", "settled decision 5", "settled decision 6", "settled decision 7", "decision 8", "decision 9" and "decision 10" a combined 48 times, and there is no table, list or appendix defining any of them in `prd.md`, `addendum.md` or `.memlog.md`. Decision 2 is never cited at all, so a reader cannot even reconstruct the set by enumeration. These are not incidental: decision 4 carries the entire Critical Alerts posture (PL-23, D-3, NG-10, R-4), decision 3 and decision 9 carry the no-conveyed-binary rule that NG-4, §8.2's raise rule and §9.4's single-validator risk all rest on, and decision 10 ("the lead developer has no iPhone and no Apple Watch") is the premise of the entire Validation Tier model. Every other ID space in this PRD — FR, NFR, SI, UJ, SM, NG, D, PL/PD/PA, R, OQ, A — has a defining table. This one does not. Content is usually recoverable from surrounding context (§8.4 restates decision 5 inline), but "settled decision 6" at §10.1 is cited with no restated content at all, and downstream architecture and epic work will cite these IDs against nothing. *Fix:* add a §0.1 or §17 Binding Decisions register with one row per decision — id, statement, date, who decided — and let every citation resolve against it. This is a one-table addition; nothing else needs to change.

## Substance over theater — strong

There are no personas, and their absence is correct: §2 is Jobs To Be Done, Non-Users, and six named user journeys. The JTBD entries are written in the user's voice with the actual grievance intact ("A stale reading presented confidently is worse than no reading"; "Know whether anything is actually watching for a low right now — and if not, why not"). §2.2's Non-Users list does real work — it forecloses closed-loop dosing ("the boundary is architectural, not a roadmap item"), forecloses the App Store, and forecloses "anyone who needs a guaranteed, unsilenceable alarm as their primary low-glucose safety net", which is a genuinely uncomfortable thing for a product to say about itself on page two.

The Vision does not swap into another PRD. "People with diabetes who use an insulin pump live inside a number that changes every five minutes" is specific to this product, and the second and third paragraphs commit to the two positions the rest of the document is built from: *where iOS delivers less, the app says so*, and *it ships as source*. §1 even names the concrete thing lost — "Android's answer to 'am I being watched right now?' is a permanent, undismissable notification. iOS has no such thing, at any price."

No NFR boilerplate survives. §6 opens with a "Measurement honesty" paragraph stating that "no NFR here is a claim about a measurement the project has not made", and then NFR-10 refuses to publish a battery figure at all rather than inherit Android's `< 5% per day`, with a four-point measurement protocol and a named owner for when one can be published. NFR-30 goes further and deletes its own earlier target: "**[NOTE FOR PM] Do not pin a count.** An earlier draft fixed the target at '253 identifiers and 113 descriptions.' Counted directly against the Android source on 2026-08-01... the 253 figure was a grep-token artifact." A PRD that catches its own fabricated precision and replaces it with a registry diff is not producing furniture.

No differentiation section exists, correctly — this is a port, and the document says so in §0.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated in §1 and every section serves it: *an honest monitor that admits its gaps is safer than a confident one that has them anyway.* You can trace it mechanically. SI-6 makes it an invariant. FR-83/FR-84 make the Coverage Claim declarative with a single expiry driving four decay mechanisms so that "silence itself becomes the alarm". FR-49/FR-50 make freshness re-evaluate on a wall-clock ticker "so a dead sensor degrades on screen on its own" — §5.3's description correctly calls this "the single most load-bearing behaviour in the section". FR-48 strips severity colour at Too Stale. FR-126 forbids the Watch from deriving its own claim. §12.1 names the Alert Floor and the Coverage Claim as non-negotiable in v1 "because they are the parts most likely to be proposed as cuts."

Prioritization follows the thesis, not ease. §12.3's sequencing puts P0.1 (Medtronic transport spike (a)) first explicitly because "a negative answer makes the entire Medtronic read layer unreachable even after a successful handshake, which changes the Driver architecture" — and it is chosen because it is answerable on macOS with no Pump, i.e. the cheapest place to invalidate an architecture. P0.2 is the Safety Constant module because "retrofitting this after parsers exist is how clamping gets introduced." P0.3 is the Required Check roster because "a gate added after the code it should have guarded is a gate that grandfathers a defect."

The counter-metrics are the strongest part of §13 and are unusually well-aimed. SM-14 forbids optimizing alert volume and names the specific means that are forbidden ("widening the Fresh window, raising an Alert Threshold default, lengthening the cooldown, or shortening the re-alarm ladder in response to noise complaints"). SM-15 forbids optimizing away honesty copy. SM-16 predicts the actual failure path — weakening a Required Check under macOS runner cost pressure — and names the permitted cost reductions instead. SM-18 forbids introducing crash-free rate, DAU, retention or funnel conversion at all. Five counter-metrics against thirteen forward metrics is the right ratio for a product whose main risk is being made more comfortable.

MVP scope kind is coherently "platform" — §12 states "The mandate is full parity. The MVP is therefore the whole product... No feature section is thinned", and the deferrals are all platform facts or cross-repository dependencies, not schedule trades.

### Findings

- **medium** Success metrics have no sample floor (§13, SM-1 / SM-2 / SM-12) — §13 is honest about *where* its data comes from (A-50: "voluntary install reports... the project cannot read a Builder's Actions logs") but never states *when* a number means anything. SM-2's target is "Median ≤ 90 minutes" over self-reported install times; with three reports that median is noise, and nothing in the metric definition says so. SM-1's "≥ 90% of reported failures classify to a named message" has the same shape. SM-12 ("Provisioning share falls release over release") is a trend claim over a denominator that may be single digits. *Fix:* add a minimum-n floor per metric (e.g. "not reported below 10 install reports in the window") or restate SM-1/SM-2/SM-12 as qualitative release-review inputs rather than numeric targets. The zero-tolerance metrics (SM-5, SM-6, SM-9, SM-10) are unaffected — a count of zero needs no denominator, and §13 already says so for SM-5.
- **low** Success metrics are cited one-directionally (§13 vs §5) — Every SM names the FRs it validates, but no FR names the SM it feeds. SM-3 through SM-10 and SM-14 through SM-18 each appear exactly once in the document. That is survivable for a metric set this operational, but it means a story author working from FR-83 has no signal that SM-5 makes any coverage-honesty defect release-blocking. *Fix:* add the SM id to the "Realizes UJ-n / Upholds SI-n" trailer on the ~20 FRs an SM names.

## Done-ness clarity — strong, and this is the document's best dimension

Every FR carries a **Consequences (testable)** block, and the consequences are bounds rather than adjectives. A scan for the usual tells returns almost nothing: three instances of "reasonable"/"reasonably" across 144,703 words, all in ordinary English usage ("reading the workflow diff is a reasonable precaution"), and zero instances of "gracefully", "user-friendly", "robust", "seamless", "scalable", "as appropriate", "TBD" or "TODO".

What is there instead: FR-13's ladder is `min(1000 × 2^min(attempt,5), 32000)` ms followed by a 120,000 ms interval "repeated without end", with a test that "drives 1,000 consecutive failed attempts against an intact pairing and asserts the loop is still running". FR-49 pins half-open boundaries at 360,000 ms and 900,000 ms and states the exact-boundary behaviour. FR-72 gives the alertability predicate as `age ≥ −60,000 && classify(max(age, 0)) == Fresh` with pinned pairs at 359,999/360,000 and −60,000/−60,001. FR-47 pins eight severity-banding boundaries by value. FR-52 pins five axis-resolution cases. FR-32 pins the Tandem `egvStatusId` gate at all five status values and the `ControlIQIOBResponse` selector at 0, 1 and unknown "against a cargo whose two IOB fields differ".

Three patterns are worth naming as strengths a downstream author should not undo:

**Single-ownership declarations.** Where a rule spans surfaces, exactly one FR owns it and the rest cross-reference. FR-52: "**This FR owns the Y-axis rule for every graph surface in the product**", and FR-133 then says "**The Y axis is not defined here.**" FR-46 owns trend-glyph provenance for the hero, the wrist, the widgets and the Lock Screen. FR-49 declares itself "the single definition of negative-age handling in this document." FR-13 owns the reconnection rule. This is the discipline that makes a 237-FR document survive editing.

**Deliberate asymmetries stated twice, on purpose.** FR-32 and FR-138 use different glucose bounds and both say so: "The asymmetry is stated in both places so a reader does not read it as a defect", with a test that "narrows the Safety Limits after ingestion and asserts that already-stored rows still read back while newly offered Driver values in the same range are dropped." Similarly FR-47's banding at exactly `low` deliberately differs from FR-58's Time-in-Range bucketing, "both behaviours are pinned by test and the divergence is recorded, not silently reconciled."

**Copy pinned against the Glossary where the two must differ.** FR-71, FR-78, FR-83, FR-87, FR-91, FR-93 and FR-106 all carry variants of "the literal string says 'server' deliberately — that is the word the product shows people for their Backend. The string is pinned by test and is not to be re-worded to the Glossary term." That resolves the single most common source of downstream copy churn before it happens.

The non-functional sections hold the same bar. §6.2's NFR-7 gives a data-ceiling table per surface; NFR-8 pins the background-wake work order ("persist the frame → advance the cursor only if it decoded (SI-8) → evaluate the Alert Floor → refresh the Coverage Claim → update wrist and widget surfaces → enqueue upload — Upload is last because it is the only step whose failure is recoverable later"). §6.7's accessibility NFRs give a registry diff as the gate rather than a count.

### Findings

- **low** `Feature-specific NFRs` occasionally use an adjective the FR body would not permit (§5.3) — "Chart rendering at the 30-day period must remain smooth under pinch-zoom and pan with the maximum retained row count". "Smooth" is undefined here; NFR-5 and NFR-7 do supply the real bound (frame budget at the 2,000/500-row caps on the oldest supported device), so the fix is a cross-reference rather than a new number. *Fix:* replace with "must hold NFR-5 at the NFR-7 ceilings", which is what the rest of the document does everywhere else.
- **low** NFR-5's frame budget is never a number (§6.2) — "completes within one frame budget on the oldest supported device" is testable in principle but leaves the reader to derive 16.67 ms from NFR-2's device list, and iPhone models in the supported set differ in refresh rate. *Fix:* state the millisecond figure and the device it is measured on, as §6.3's NFR-10 does for battery.

## Scope honesty — strong

§11 Non-Goals is a 17-row table where every row states *why it is permanent* rather than merely that it is out. NG-1 grounds itself in the mechanism ("SI-1 is architectural, not a scope decision; it is enforced by the closed six-member Capability set (FR-30), read-only-by-construction (FR-31) and the Driver protocol public-interface snapshot gate (FR-205) — not by policy"). NG-6 explains why no-telemetry can never be reversed: "there is nothing on the receiving end and never will be: the project operates no service (NG-5) and holds no account any build could report to, so this is not a toggle anyone can flip back." §11's header enforces the discipline the rubric asks for: "Each row is permanent for this product, not a v1 cut. Anything with a revisit condition lives in §12, not here."

De-scoping is proposed openly, never done silently. NG-17 removes the calibration Capability and states the full consequence: "the published capability list narrows, and a future CGM Driver needing calibration is a PRD change with its own safety review rather than a slot already open." §12.2's D-1 states the user-visible cost of deferring APNs in bold ("Backend-generated alerts — including caregiver alerts — do **not** reach a suspended or terminated app in v1"). Addendum §2.1 records the three rejected alternatives for server-generated alerts and marks per-deployment APNs as "**The right eventual design** — deferred only because it requires work in the platform repository, which this PRD cannot discharge."

The Parity Ledger is the scope-honesty instrument and it is complete. 72 forced-loss rows (PL-70 retired with an explicit note), 46 deliberate divergences, 18 additive substitutes — every PL row carries a "Where the user is told" column, and §7's own rule is that "a forced loss with no disclosure surface is a defect." That rule caught a real gap and produced FR-237, which §7.4 documents rather than hides: "It was created to close a real defect — five forced-loss rows named a disclosure surface no FR required to exist."

**Open-items density.** 87 `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, 51 live open questions, 63 `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts — roughly 0.37, 0.21 and 0.27 per FR. On a 237-FR green-light-to-build PRD that would normally be a blocker. It is not one here, for three reasons that the document earns rather than asserts: (a) only seven questions are marked **(blocking)** and each names the specific work it gates (OQ-45 gates the ATS plist baseline, OQ-50 gates FR-182's provisioning workflow and §12.2 D-4 says it "must be decided **before** the FR-182 provisioning workflow is built"); (b) §16's Assumptions Index round-trips completely — all 56 A-ids resolve, six are explicitly retired with the ruling that retired them named, and every one carries its owning FR; (c) a large share of the assumptions are genuinely about the external world (Apple's device support lists, the Backend's inference ceiling, whether a 780G's discovery filter accepts an iOS advertisement), not about product intent the PM could simply state.

§2.3's blanket assumption on the journeys is the right kind of honesty: "The journeys below are authored from the Android product's behavior and the constraints of the target platform, not from user interviews... Correct or replace any that don't match your intent."

### Findings

- **low** No inline `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` callouts exist (0 across the document) — the rubric looks for these where an omission could be silently assumed. In practice §11's 17-row table plus §12.2's 9-row deferral table plus per-FR `**Out of Scope:**` blocks (present on ~40 FRs) do the work more thoroughly than callouts would, and several Out-of-Scope entries carry their own revisit condition (FR-67's user-imported audio). Recorded so a reader does not mistake the absence for an oversight; no fix needed.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This is a chain-top PRD feeding UX, architecture and epic planning, so this dimension carries real weight — and it is the only one where the document falls short of its own standard.

The strengths are substantial. The Glossary (§3) is present, organised into four bands, and binding by declaration in §0: "Introducing a synonym anywhere in this document is a defect." Term discipline holds up under measurement — **Coverage Claim** appears 106 times, **Alert Floor** 98, **Glucose Reading** 86, **Required Check** 110, **Backend-optional mode** 70, with no competing synonym in play. FR-83 explicitly bans the near-miss: "'Server Active' is not a state name anywhere in code, wire payload or documentation", and the only occurrence of that string in the document is the prohibition itself.

ID continuity in the primary spaces is clean. FR-1 through FR-237: 237 headings, no gaps, no duplicates, no reference above 237. NFR-1 through NFR-34 contiguous. SI-1 through SI-12. UJ-1 through UJ-6, all six with named protagonists (Sam, Priya, Marcus, Dana, Ren, Alex) carrying age, device and context inline, and all six actually load-bearing rather than decorative (UJ-1 cited 56 times, UJ-6 35, UJ-4 27). PL/PD/PA complete with the single retirement documented. NG-1 through NG-17, D-1 through D-9, A-1 through A-56 with six documented retirements.

Sections do read alone. Every feature section opens with a **Description**, closes with **Feature-specific NFRs** and **Notes**, and cross-references by Glossary term and FR id rather than "see above". The `See FR-n` convention is used consistently and the referenced FR always exists.

What pulls this to *adequate* is that three ID spaces break, and two of them break inside self-audit passages that assert they do not.

### Findings

- **medium** Dangling risk IDs and an undocumented gap in §14 — §14 defines R-1 through R-13, R-20, R-21 and R-22. R-14 through R-19 and R-23 do not exist, yet **R-16 is cited twice** (§13 SM-16: "the predicted failure path under macOS runner cost pressure (R-16)"; §15 OQ-49: "a private upstream would put the required-check set under cost pressure (R-16)") and **R-23 is cited once** (§13 SM-11: "a green in-repo gate is not evidence of publication (R-23)"). §14's preamble says IDs "are stable and are never renumbered", which anticipates gaps — but unlike §15 and §16, which both enumerate exactly which IDs were retired and why, §14 lists nothing. A PM triaging risk from SM-16 has a citation that resolves to nothing. *Fix:* either restore R-16 (CI cost pressure on the Required Check roster) and R-23 (published-page verification vs green in-repo gate) as rows — both are real risks the document clearly reasons about — or add a retirement note to §14 in the same form §15 uses, and repoint SM-11, SM-16 and OQ-49 at whatever absorbed them.
- **medium** §15's self-audit claim is false by its own stated rule — §15 asserts: "The ten retired identifiers are **OQ-1 – OQ-6, OQ-8, OQ-28, OQ-30 and OQ-44**. Those ten gaps are exactly the ten struck questions above and nothing else is absent: the live set is the 51 questions below, running OQ-7 through OQ-63 with no other break. A reader finding a gap not on that list has found a defect." The live set is 51 questions, correctly — but it runs **OQ-7 through OQ-62**, and **OQ-57 is a gap not on the retirement list**. Both halves of the claim are wrong. This is low-impact mechanically and high-impact reputationally: the document's method is to make auditable claims about itself, and this is the one place a reader can check the method and find it failed. *Fix:* correct the upper bound to OQ-62 and either restore OQ-57 or add it to the retired list with the ruling that closed it.
- **low** Glossary drift on `Pump` in requirement prose (§2.3, §5.1, §14) — §0 sets the bar at "Introducing a synonym anywhere in this document is a defect", and the document is scrupulous about protecting deliberate copy divergences ("the literal string says 'server' deliberately... pinned by test"). Four uses of lowercase "the pump" fall outside quoted copy and outside that protection: §2.3 UJ-4 ("whenever the pump is in range... if the pump has forgotten this phone"), §5.1's Description at line 170 ("states plainly that the pump no longer trusts this phone"), §14 R-1 ("the advertisement the pump's matcher was reverse-engineered against") and §16 A-2 ("the pump-side pairing-code timeout"). *Fix:* capitalise, or add the same "deliberate plain-language usage" annotation the document already applies to "server", "sensor reading" and "BG".

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product. This is a brownfield full-parity port of a safety-relevant consumer health app with a real UX surface, feeding downstream UX/architecture/epics, distributed under a model that makes the *Builder* a second protagonist alongside the patient. The document handles all four of those correctly:

**Consumer product with meaningful UX → UJs are load-bearing, and they are.** Six journeys, each with a named protagonist, a climax, a resolution and an edge case — and the edge cases carry the safety content. UJ-2's is flagged inline as "and this one is a product requirement, not a footnote: had Priya's phone been on silent, this alarm would have been inaudible, and the app has no way to detect that." UJ-3's is the 90-day expiry: "ninety days later that build stops launching — a total loss of monitoring, silently, unless he rebuilds."

**Brownfield → existing-code references are specific and the new/existing boundary is drawn.** The document cites Android artefacts by name and behaviour throughout (`DexPluginLoader`, `RestrictedContext`'s seven-service allowlist, `versionCode = major*1_000_000 + minor*10_000 + patch`, `RELEASE_SIGNER_SHA256=55f0d0...`, `fallbackToDestructiveMigration()`, the `[0, 500, 200, 500, 200, 500]` vibration pattern) and marks what is net-new rather than ported: FR-35/FR-36 note that "`plugins/example` is **not** listed in Android's `settings.gradle.kts`", and addendum §5 lists three things "Verified absent from the Android client, and therefore **not** hidden scope inside the parity mandate." §5.2's note that "the Android source carries a three-way API-version drift: code says 5, docs say 2, the example plugin says 1... Port from the Kotlin source, never from the Android documentation" is exactly the brownfield warning this shape needs.

**Two-audience product → both are served without either being an afterthought.** §2.1 gives the maintainer two JTBD entries, UJ-3 and UJ-6 are Builder/contributor journeys, and §5.10–§5.12 (57 FRs) exist because the build pipeline and the published docs *are* the delivery mechanism under fork-and-build — §12.1 makes the point sharply: "the install runbook validated end to end by someone who did not write it (FR-223), because under fork-and-build the runbook *is* the delivery mechanism."

**Chain-top → §8, §9 and §10 are the right three additions.** §9's Validation Tier model in particular does something a generic PRD would not: §9.2 "What Tier 1 falsely passes" enumerates six properties on which a green Simulator run is a *category error* rather than an absence of evidence, and binds the consequence ("no Tier 1 result may be cited as evidence for a Verification Status, ever").

The document is not over-formalised. §5.11 and §5.12 are capability-spec-shaped with almost no UJ density, which is right for CI machinery and published pages. §6 is system-wide quality attributes with explicit anti-duplication rules.

### Findings

- **low** Two feature areas are journey-orphaned (§5.5 FR-89–FR-92, §5.6 FR-105–FR-113) — 87 of 237 FRs carry no UJ reference. For §5.8's storage internals, §5.11's gates and §5.12's docs that is correct shape. Two cases are not: AI Chat has no journey at all, and the insulin half of §5.5 (Insulin Summary, delivered-insulin honesty, Recent Boluses, glucose-at-event) sits under UJ-5, which is entirely about meals. The PRD flags both itself and declines to invent a journey, which is the right instinct — but of the two, the insulin one matters more, because FR-90 carries a genuine safety rule with no narrative anchor: "No layout places `mealUnits`, `correctionUnits` and delivered `units` in a way that reads as an equation." That is precisely the kind of rule a designer without the reasoning will "simplify" into a tidy summed row. *Fix:* add one journey for reviewing what insulin was actually delivered (the PRD's own §5.5 note proposes it), and leave AI Chat journey-less as §5.6's note argues.

## Mechanical notes

- **Glossary drift.** Clean, with the one exception filed above (lowercase "pump" in four prose sites). The document's handling of deliberate copy/Glossary divergence is a model worth preserving: "server", "sensor reading" and "BG" are all shipped user-facing strings that differ from the Glossary term, and each is annotated inline, pinned by test, and marked not-to-be-reworded. `plugin` appears 49 times, all in Android-context references where the Glossary's **Driver** is correctly the iOS term.
- **ID continuity.** FR-1–237 contiguous, unique, no out-of-range references. NFR-1–34, SI-1–12, UJ-1–6, NG-1–17, D-1–9, PA-1–18, PD-1–46 all contiguous. PL runs 1–72 with PL-70 retired and its retirement note cross-referenced from §7.4. Broken: §14's R-space (R-14–R-19 and R-23 absent, R-16 and R-23 cited) and §15's OQ-space (OQ-57 an undocumented gap; the stated upper bound OQ-63 does not exist). Both filed above.
- **Cross-reference resolution.** Every `See FR-n` sampled resolves. Every SM's "Validates" column resolves. Every PL row's "Where the user is told" column names an FR that exists — including FR-237, which was created specifically to give five such rows a real target, and §7.4 documents that creation rather than quietly renumbering.

- **UJ protagonists.** All six named, all six carrying context inline (age, device, situation). No floating UJs.
- **Required sections for the stakes.** Present and then some: Safety Invariant Register before any feature, Parity Ledger, per-device verification matrix with a raise rule, validation tier model with a false-pass table, security gate substitution table, risks with named owners, non-goals, deferrals with revisit conditions, success metrics with counter-metrics, assumptions index. The three product-specific artefacts (§8, §9, §10) are the ones a generic template would not have produced and are among the strongest content in the document.
